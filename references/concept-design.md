# Concept Design

## The important shift

Do not begin with “What surreal thing should I add?”

Begin with:

**“What is already happening in this photograph, and how can its logic be rewritten?”**

## Three-proposition method

Before editing, write three directions.

### Proposition format
- **Anchor:** what must remain real
- **Purification:** what becomes quieter/simpler
- **Relationship rewrite:** what changes between existing elements
- **Visual consequence:** what the viewer sees
- **Believability:** why it still feels photographed

### Example: person holding soft-serve near lotus pond

Weak first association:
- make the ice cream huge

Better directions:
1. Let the spiral geometry of the ice cream migrate into the lotus field and pavilion roofs.
2. Let the green ice cream and lotus leaves share material/texture so the object appears harvested from the pond.
3. Let the cone’s dark lattice become an architectural/grid logic that reorganizes the waterfront.

The source object remains important, but its *role* changes.

## Reject cliché quickly

Be suspicious of:
- giant props
- mystical doors
- floating moons
- generic fog
- isolated house on hill
- mirror portal
- impossible stairs

They can still work, but only when derived from the source relationship rather than added automatically.

## The “one-line gimmick” warning

If the whole concept is completely explained by:
> “I made the ice cream huge.”

it is probably not deep enough.

Prefer:
> “The soft-serve spiral becomes the organizing geometry of the landscape while the original object remains in the foreground.”

The second version creates a system, not a trick.

## Visual thesis examples

- A person is not in a forest; their clothing and the forest are the same growth system.
- Hair is not blowing in wind; hair becomes the spatial mass that replaces half the sky.
- A crowd is not walking through vegetation; the crowd becomes a graphic organism inside the vegetation.
- A road is not a road; it becomes the only white river-like line that structures the terrain.
- A building is not placed in nature; the nature around it behaves as though it was grown to frame the building.
